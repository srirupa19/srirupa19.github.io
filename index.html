About Me

Hi, I am Srirupa (IRC: sriru:matrix.org). I am currently a second-year Master's student in Computer Science at the Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences, NYU, interested in pursuing research in Computer Science. My research interests focus on the intersection of deep learning, computer vision, and reinforcement learning.

I'm doing my thesis at NYU with Prof. Davi Geiger, studying how Vision Transformers organize information internally across image patches and feature channels, and how these internal structures emerge over the course of training. Our work draws on tools from information theory and causal analysis to understand which components shape a model's representations.

Alongside my courses, I contributed to digiKam (KDE) as part of Google Summer of Code 2026, building an LLM-assisted natural-language search feature that maps plain-English queries into structured search criteria (mentored by Gilles Caulier). Before joining NYU, I worked as a Software Engineer at Morgan Stanley (2023–2025), where I modernized legacy ETL pipelines, led large-scale data migration to Snowflake, and built full-stack application features using Python and Angular. In the summer of 2023, I also contributed to Krita (KDE) as part of Google Summer of Code under the mentorship of Emmet O'Neill, redesigning Krita's Bundle Creator, a tool that enables users to create compressed files called Resource Bundles, which house collections of resources, and adding the capability to edit these bundles.

Before my graduation, I worked with Prof. Amit Konar and Prof. Sanjoy Kumar Saha on research projects that deepened my knowledge of deep learning and signal processing, including work on motor imagery classification from EEG signals. As a painter with a passion for software development, I actively contributed to Krita while also engaging myself in learning and practicing digital painting. During my undergraduate years, I had the opportunity to intern at Simon Fraser University in the summer of 2022 through the Mitacs Globalink Fellowship, where I gained research experience in EEG-based brain age classification. I graduated from Jadavpur University, India, in 2023 with a Bachelor's degree in Electrical Engineering.

Research

M.S. Thesis: Mechanistic Interpretability of Vision Transformers
New York University, Fall 2026 – Present
Supervisor: Prof. Davi Geiger

Characterizing how Vision Transformers organize information internally, with a focus on the high-norm outlier tokens that emerge on uninformative background patches. Combining causal and information-theoretic analyses across pretrained ViTs to explain why these tokens arise and what downstream computation depends on them, with implications for quantization and model compression.

Spatial Credit Assignment for RL Post-Training of Image Editors
Course Project, Computer Vision (NYU), Fall 2026
Supervisor: Prof. Saining Xie

Analyzed spatial credit assignment in reinforcement-learning post-training of flow-matching image editors, where image-level rewards induce unintended changes outside the edited region. Developed a credit-assignment framework that improves background fidelity while preserving instruction adherence.

Reward-Hacking-Resistant RL for Vision-Language Models
Course Project, RLFM (NYU), Fall 2026
Supervisor: Prof. Sherry Yang

Examined reward hacking in RL post-training of vision-language models under partial verifiers, where policies exploit unverifiable failure modes such as object omission. Developed a theoretically grounded, compute-efficient reward formulation that mitigates such exploitation across grounding and scene-understanding benchmarks.

Ensembles of Generative Policies for Exploration in Online RL
Course Project, Machine Learning (NYU), Spring 2026
Supervisor: Prof. Rajesh Ranganath

Characterized, theoretically and empirically, how ensembles of diffusion and flow-based policies in online RL collapse to identical behavior, limiting exploration. Proposed an actor-critic framework that preserves policy diversity and leverages it for exploration, yielding gains on continuous-control MuJoCo benchmarks.

Self-Supervised Learning Using Vision Transformers and MoCo v3
Course Project, Deep Learning (NYU), Fall 2025
Anagha Radhakrishna Palandye*, Srirupa Datta*, Vijay Jayawant Harkare*
Supervisor: Prof. Alfredo Canziani, Prof. Yann LeCun

Explored self-supervised visual representation learning under strict capacity and resolution constraints (≤100M parameters, 96×96 inputs, random initialization). Pretrained Vision Transformers with momentum-contrastive learning (MoCo v3) on a curated 1.7M-image corpus, contributing a ViT-Tiny to the best-performing heterogeneous ensemble and scaling a ViT-Small to 42.6% / 74.5% / 48.9% linear-probe accuracy on CUB-200, MiniImageNet and SUN397.
